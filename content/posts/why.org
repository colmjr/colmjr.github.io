I intent this quick blog post just to be on my motivations with learning and doing stuff, but I have a few draft articles on a (I think?) original proof in linear algebra that I made up and formalized with AI(I say formalized as in typst formatting, just because I am too stupid and don't care enough just yet to spend time learning how to write typst beyond basic syntax, but I did already have the entire proof including a_11 == 0 case assumptions already on paper), and I have a draft on the use and implementation of ADTs in different languages, but I am not sure whether that one will be finalized.
I am first and foremost a religious person, specifically a Catholic. And so, most or all of my motivation for doing stuff really comes from the belief in God and the parable of the talents and other stuff as well, but most of what I am going to say stems from that. It is a shame that I have not the Bible yet, and I have only heard it in church, and I really should. But anyways, I view the declarative style of "creation", whether it is in math or functional programming, to be descended from divine. I do not deny the fact that some imperative processes can also create great things and be beautiful for the eye to see, but just as God used a "declarative" style in Genesis to create the world, a similar but of course worse (as the products of what we make are largely earthly) way of doing "let there be light!", we can declare things into existence through no interface with the real world in ideal circumstances. Mathematics is an example of being completely detached from the world, as verification and proof are independent on anything that happens in the world. Even if Earth was gone one day, under a set of assumptions(I will assume for now that these are perfect), you will always get the same "results" of proof. Functional programming is slightly different, as the nature of compiling into machine code means that FP languages need to turn their declarative style into imperative for things to actually "happen". In my view, we can view this as humans consciously creating an interface between FP and imperative instructions, but for example theoretically lambda calculus would run the same way in an idealized environment. Now how this ties into Christianity is my view that as everything is declarative, everything that we see that is beautiful with FP and mathematics and the like has already existed since the beginning of the world because as I said in the beginning, this is independent of any wordly influence, and these are only faint apparitions compared to the glory of God. They were creations of God, just as we were, but in a different sense. So when I am learning math and FP and the like, I look at it as learning about the amazing constructs that God has given us to work with, and embracing the glory of God(whether that makes sense).
